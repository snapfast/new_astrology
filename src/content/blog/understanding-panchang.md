Vedic timekeeping is rooted in the eternal wisdom of the **Panchang**, a traditional Hindu astrological calendar that analyzes the subtle cosmic energies influencing each day. The word *Panchang* originates from Sanskrit, where *Pancha* means five and *Anga* means limbs or attributes.

## The Five Limbs of Panchang

1. **Tithi (Lunar Day)**
   A Tithi represents the distance between the Sun and the Moon in 12-degree increments. There are 30 Tithis in a lunar month, divided into the waxing phase (*Shukla Paksha*) and waning phase (*Krishna Paksha*).

2. **Vara (Solar Day)**
   Vara is the day of the week ruled by a specific deity and planet:
   - Sunday (Ravivara) – Sun
   - Monday (Somavara) – Moon
   - Tuesday (Mangalavara) – Mars
   - Wednesday (Budhavara) – Mercury
   - Thursday (Guruvara) – Jupiter
   - Friday (Shukravara) – Venus
   - Saturday (Shanivara) – Saturn

3. **Nakshatra (Lunar Mansion)**
   The ecliptic is divided into 27 Nakshatras (star constellations). The Moon takes approximately 27.3 days to orbit the Earth, spending about one day in each Nakshatra.

4. **Yoga (Sol-Lunar Combination)**
   Yoga is derived from the combined angular distance of the Sun and Moon. There are 27 Yogas, each possessing unique spiritual and material qualities.

5. **Karana (Half Lunar Day)**
   A Karana is equal to half a Tithi (6-degree angular difference). There are 11 Karanas in total, consisting of 7 moveable and 4 fixed Karanas.

## Why Consult the Panchang?

Checking the daily Panchang helps individuals select auspicious times (*Muhurta*) for major life events, such as business launches, weddings, travel, and spiritual ceremonies, while avoiding unfavorable periods like **Rahu Kalam**.
